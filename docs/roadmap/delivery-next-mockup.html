        What is wrong today

        • The view switch changes nothing. StageHeader draws Business · IT · Management on every tool (components/StageHeader.tsx:54–57); delivery/page.tsx never reads the view — it only links to ?view=management (:680, :1193). The same page for everyone, under a header that promises three.
        • No answer, and the next step comes after the anchor bar. The first screen is the lead, a metaline behind "Details" (DeliveryObjectPage.tsx:39–58), four facets — Handover · Audit pack · Design target · Quality — and a status line in four dots, "Run · Decision · Receipts · Engine" (delivery/page.tsx:723–726, lib/handover.ts:1126–1155). The one sentence that says what to do, Next step · rule-based, stands below the stale notice and the anchor bar, inside the main column (delivery/page.tsx:742). No sentence says whether the program can be handed over.
        • "Handed over" means "downloaded". The Handover facet reads Handed over as soon as the audit pack was sealed and downloaded (lib/handover.ts:1065), with a proven chip. A download is not a handover, and its own basis text says so ("delivery is not acceptance").
        • Two chains with the same four names, and different verdicts. The "Evidence chain" card draws Requirement → Decision → Receipt → Delivery artefact from the nine project links (delivery/page.tsx:773–958, lib/handover.ts:834–842); the side column draws "The pack's own chain" with the same four names from the signed fields (delivery/page.tsx:1369–1383, lib/evidence-chain.ts:28–36). The first can say "on record" where the second says "not determined" — for the same link.
        • Signed vs. not signed is spread over four places. The Artefacts header says "review material — not signed" (:969), each artefact card repeats it in its own words (:978, :1044, :1155), the side column has "What a signature covers" with three lists (:1356–1388), and the pack's contents sit in an accordion (:1334–1352). Nowhere does one table say, statement by statement, what stands behind it.
        • The audit pack sits in the side column, but the anchor bar points to it. id="audit-pack" is inside <aside> (:1217–1221); on a phone the side column stacks under the main column, so the bar's fourth item jumps past everything. The bar's items are Evidence chain · Still needed · Artefacts · Audit pack · Stakeholder slides (lib/page-anchors.ts:169–186), drawn in the old style.
        • Six download buttons of equal weight. Bundle, Markdown, Developer guide, BPMN, audit pack and the next step's own pack button (:983, :1100, :1129, :1163, :1282, :757) — and a bundle without the BPMN, the specification or the slides.
        • Checking a pack happens elsewhere, in other words. Verification is a separate page, /verify-pack (:1328), whose verdicts are Title Case — "Authenticity & Integrity Verified", "Verification Failed" (lib/audit-pack-verify.ts:580–593). The delivery package can be checked against the pack (lib/delivery-package-verify.ts, scripts/verify-pack.mjs --package), but Delivery never says so.
        • The next step ignores the new tool order. handoverNextStep walks design → transformation → documentation → testing (lib/handover.ts:749) and puts the cost scenario after the seal (:802–806). Since ADR-128 Economics comes before Design and Design works from its decision.
        • The slides are a full-width section under everything (:1434–1441), reached from a card's "View slides" button that scrolls the page (:1073).
        • The program decision and the test result are not in the pack — and the page does not say it. 07-user-attested.md carries the target, the sign-off and a testing closure only (lib/audit-pack.ts:916–930); the status line nevertheless lists "Decision · confirmed" and "Receipts" next to "Run · signed" as if they were one kind of fact.

        For the owner — decisions L1–L13

        Recommendation: build L1–L10 and L12–L13 as one Delivery step before the 3.5 release, on one page for every reader. The page opens with whether the program can be handed over, in one or two sentences, and the one next step; four facets say what is on record, what the package holds, whether the tests ran and whether the audit pack is sealed. What is signed and what is not is said once, in a table. Fingerprints, signatures, the check of a pack and the abapGit import stay on the page, quietly. No engine change, no new stored field, no rules change. L11 (the program decision and the test result into the sealed file) is a pack-format change — recommended, as its own later step.
        1. L1 One page for every reader — recommended, following ADR-126 (1), decided for Economics, Documentation and Transformation. Delivery's three readers need the same facts at different depth, not different content: the business reader asks "can it go?", the manager "what is proven and what is open?", the developer "which files, which fingerprints, how do I import it?". Depth folds; nothing differs by view. So no switch, the quiet note in its place, the view kept in the address. Alternative (the Design rule, ADR-126 (2)) only if you want a Management variant that shows nothing but the answer and the proof table — not recommended: it would be the same page with sections hidden.
        2. L2 The answer first. "Can <program> be handed over?" — Yes, Not yet or Stop, one or two sentences naming what is missing, and the next step on the right. Four facets: On record (the nine links of the chain, as a bar), The package (six parts, who wrote them), Tests (where they ran), Audit pack (sealed or not, checked or not). The status line and the metaline go; their facts move to "Built on". Recommended.
        3. L3 One table: "What is proven, and what is not". Statement by statement — findings, source, recommendation, model participation, limits, target and sign-off, program decision, test result, package, grade, AI text — with what stands behind it and one of four words: Signed by the server, Sealed — your word, Not signed, Never signed. Replaces the Evidence chain boxes, "The pack's own chain" and "What a signature covers"; the nine links stay one click deeper in the side column. Recommended.
        4. L4 No "Handed over". The Handover facet's Handed over (lib/handover.ts:1065) goes. The page says Ready and Sealed on …; handover, acceptance, transport and go-live are named as the reader's, outside the product. Recommended.
        5. L5 "Before go-live" in three groups. Before handover (what handoverStillNeeded lists — it blocks, amber count in the bar), Travels open, named (calls without a released successor, custom tables without a home, rules without a decision — they do not block, STATUS.md names them), and Yours, in your system (activation, transport, go-live). One action each. Recommended.
        6. L6 The package as six parts. Code for abapGit, tests, documentation, process as BPMN, requirements specification, stakeholder slides — each with its author chips (engine, AI-generated, your answers), its state and its files with SHA-256 on a click; one Download the package ZIP with all six plus STATUS.md and the manifest (today the bundle has no BPMN, specification or slides). The slides preview in a drawer. Recommended.
        7. L7 The audit pack in the main column, and the check on the page. Seal state, signatures, the 13 files folded, and Check a pack or package: the verifier of /verify-pack and lib/delivery-package-verify.ts, run in this browser on a file the reader drops; verdicts in sentence case. /verify-pack stays for readers without an account; the offline command is shown. Recommended.
        8. L8 "Import into SAP", folded. The abapGit steps the package's README already carries, said once on the page, for developers. Recommended.
        9. L9 The next step follows the tool order — decided order, recommended rule. The order Analyze → Economics → Design → Transformation → Documentation → Testing → Delivery is decided (ADR-128 (5)). handoverNextStep (lib/handover.ts:749) walks it too: Economics before Design, no longer "after the seal". Recommended.
        10. L10 The grade is named, never signed. The target line says "aims at clean core level A"; the side column "Never in the signed pack" lists the Level A–D grade, AI-written text, generated files, the views and the cost figures. Recommended.
        11. L11 Program decision and test result into the sealed file — later, own step. Today neither is in the pack (lib/audit-pack.ts:916–930). Adding both to 07-user-attested.md as your word (the decision with its date and account; the imported result file's digest) lets an auditor see them sealed. A pack-format version: lib/audit-pack-build.ts, -canonical.ts, both verifiers. Recommended, not in this step; until then the table says "on the project, not in the pack".
        12. L12 Demo: nothing leaves — kept. No download, no pack, no check, absent rather than disabled (tests/demo-project.spec.ts); the page says what a real handover would hold. Recommended.
        13. L13 The shared "On this page" bar — decided (ADR-126 (3), tmp/ANCHOR-BAR-SPEC.md): Before go-live (amber count) · In the package · What is proven · Audit pack · Import into SAP · Timeline. Amends ADR-091's Delivery list.
