              What is wrong today

              • No answer. The page opens on the tool's lead (route-dependent, page.tsx:1351–1363), a meta line (1365) and four tiles — Scenarios · Last run · Check by hand · Tenant tests (components/testing/TestingHeader.tsx:228–315) — with a status line of three more labels (Suite · Runner · Tenant, 329; values at page.tsx:1393–1404). No sentence says whether the program is tested, against what, where, or what to do next. On an empty project even the tiles are gone (hasTestingRecord, page.tsx:1376).
              • Steps as a page structure. The body is a numbered flow — 1 Write the scenarios (1446), 2 Run them against mocks (1585), the SAP result (1848), 3 Check by hand (1882, state always "open"), then Scenarios (1933), the console (1975) and the tenant (2033). The bar repeats the steps: "Write · Run · Your SAP result · Check by hand · Scenarios" (lib/page-anchors.ts:150–165). A manager has to read the procedure to find the result.
              • Step 2 carries everything. Inside one card: the route notice, the ABAP Unit evidence, two failure strips, the pipeline counters (1695), the evidence card with coverage, invariant and mutation probe (1708), the closure card (1726), "Results" (1736), a stub warning (1744), "AI Test Analysis" (1758) and a "Run report — Automation report for …" with a pie chart and a "Results by category" bar chart (1773–1838). The verdict is in the middle of the tool, not at the top.
              • Scenarios are listed by number, not by rule. The list (ScenarioList.tsx) shows rows with a verdict; the rule a scenario tests is inside each row's details, and the rule coverage ("n of m rules") is inside the evidence card in step 2. A business reader cannot see which rules are tested without opening rows.
              • A model's guess is the biggest figure on the side. "Coverage estimate" shows the testing model's percentage in title size (page.tsx:2817–2840), labelled "not a measured coverage" in small print — beside the measured rule coverage that sits hidden in step 2.
              • 737 lines of tenant administration inside a test tool. "Tenant connection — check only" (page.tsx:2033–2769): BYOT request, saved connection, four auth methods, setup guide, OData metadata read, the last check — plus a side card (2779–2812). None of it tests the program; running tests on a tenant is locked (LIVE_TEST_EXECUTION).
              • The bar is not the shared one. PageAnchorBar with className="mb-3" (page.tsx:1420): 12 px to the first section, the spec says 24; labels are steps, not what a reader looks for.
              • Word drift. "Regenerate Suite" (1471), "View Module Code" / "View ABAP Unit Class" (1997) in title case on a sentence-case product; "AI Test Analysis" as an h3.
              • The demo is three "Not in the demo" cards. components/demo/DemoTesting.tsx:64–92: steps 1 and 2 say only what is missing; the engine's real counter-checks (rule by rule) are not shown in the demo at all.

              What is good and is kept

              • The route rule: nothing here runs ABAP Unit (lib/test-runnability.ts), said before any click.
              • The result from your SAP system — JUnit XML import or a confirmation, server-written, bound to run, code, class and scenario list (ADR-075). Never green, never "proven".
              • Rule tracing and the honest per-case words — unchanged, passed · expectation not verified, failed · rule marked to change, defect (ADR-095, ADR-100); the origin check (ADR-071); the mutation probe and the shared-selection check on the sandbox route.
              • A failed or impossible run is on record; testing can be closed without a pass, "not verified" (ADR-094).
              • The hand checks (constructs the engine did not judge) and the counter-checks for the business — both from the engine.
              • The testing phase words in lib/workflow-steps.ts:985–1110 (Not started · Test draft · Passed · mock · Imported · passed · Failures · your system · Run failed · Closed · not verified) — the answer reuses them.

              For the owner — decisions S1–S16

              Recommendation: build S2–S13 and S16 as one Testing step before the 3.5 release, on one page for every reader (S1). The page opens with the answer — tested or not, against which rules, with what result, where it ran, what is not proven and the one next step — readable by a business person, a manager and a developer. Everything a developer needs stays on the page, folded. No engine change, no new stored field, no rules change.
              1. S1 One page for every reader. Testing has no Business, IT or Management variant and shows no view switch; "One page for every reader — business, IT and management" stands in its place (ADR-126 (1), as on Economics, Documentation and Transformation). Why not Design's rule (ADR-126 (2)): the three readers ask the same question — is it tested, where, what is missing — and differ only in depth; today's page reads no view at all, so the switch changes nothing. A business tester's part (acceptance checks) and a developer's part (code, output) are sections of one page, not views. Recommended; needs your word for Testing.
              2. S2 The answer first. The question "Is <program> tested?", one or two sentences per state (not started · written, not run · ran and passed · ran with failures · run gave no result · closed, not verified · demo), the next step beside it, and four facets: Scenarios (who wrote them), Business rules with a case, Result (always with where), Not proven. The sentences come from the phase words of lib/workflow-steps.ts, so Testing, the tool bar and Delivery say the same. Recommended.
              3. S3 Every result says where it ran. Three places, named the same everywhere: here — Clean-Core.io's isolated runner against SAP mocks; your SAP system (imported or confirmed, ADR-075); your tenant from here — locked. "Tested" and "passed" never appear without the place; a pass against mocks reads "not proof in your system". The pie chart, the "Results by category" bar chart and the "Automation report" go. Recommended.
              4. S4 Results by business rule. The 12 rules of the signed analysis in their plain sentence with their decision (keep · change · open), the cases under each; a rule without a case is shown as such; failing rules first and open. Each case says who set the expected result — Engine, from the original or AI, not verified — and what a failure means in ADR-095/100's words (defect · rule marked to change · assertion written by the model). Recommended.
              5. S5 "Where it ran" — one section. The three places side by side with the active one marked, the run record under them (file, SHA-256, system, account, time — or the sandbox receipt), and the actions: on the ABAP Cloud route Upload the ABAP Unit result and Confirm without a file with a four-step how-to; on the CAP route Run against SAP mocks. Replaces steps 2 and "Record the result from your SAP system". Recommended.
              6. S6 "Not proven" — one list. Rules without a case, the places no generated test covers (today's "Check by hand"), expectations only the model stated, what only your system can show (the testing model's list, AI chip), and on the CAP route "not run in your SAP system at all". One action each; amber count in the bar. Recommended.
              7. S7 Acceptance checks for a business tester. Today's counter-checks (CounterChecks.tsx, from the engine) get their own section with a plain table — rule, try this, expect, prepare — five shown, the rest on a click. Nothing records them (no stored field). Recommended; recording a key-user acceptance as on the key-user route is a later option.
              8. S8 For developers, folded. The test class (ABAP Unit) or suite (node:test) with Download for ADT and Export Excel, the run output, the origin check (ADR-071), the class check (lib/testing-abap-unit.ts), and on the sandbox route the mutation probe, the shared-selection check and the stubbed packages. A case's Code opens a drawer: the original's lines beside the test method. Recommended.
              9. S9 The tenant connection moves to Settings. The 737 lines of BYOT setup and checks (page.tsx:2033–2769) become "Your SAP test system" in Settings; Testing keeps one line, "Tests on your tenant from here: locked", with the link. Recommended; alternative: keep it as a fold at the end of Testing.
              10. S10 The model's coverage estimate leaves the side column. Replaced by the counted rule coverage (facet 2). Recommended; alternative: keep it inside "For developers" with its AI chip.
              11. S11 Closing without a passing run stays (ADR-094), offered beside the failures in the answer's next step; the reasons and "Closed · not verified" unchanged. Recommended.
              12. S12 The demo tests nothing and says so in the answer, then shows what is real: the engine's 12 rules, the acceptance checks and the places no test would cover. One action: upload your own program. Recommended.
              13. S13 Key-user decision on the same page. When the decision is key-user extensibility, "What was tested" lists the step list's acceptance checks and their recorded results; the rest of the page is the same. Replaces the separate early return at page.tsx:1301–1333. Recommended.
              14. S14 The shared "On this page" bar — decided (ADR-126 (3)): What was tested · Where it ran · Not proven (amber) · Acceptance checks · For developers. Amends ADR-091's Testing list.
              15. S15 Tool bar order — decided (ADR-128 (5)): Analyze → Economics → Design → Transformation → Documentation → Testing → Delivery.
              16. S16 Writing the scenarios in the same page. While the model writes, the answer reads "Writing the scenarios — 5 of 8 rules done" with Cancel as the next step, as Transformation does (T11); today's progress box under the button goes. Recommended.
